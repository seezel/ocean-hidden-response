Know what is behind the view.

These are historical satellite estimates. A regional association between temperature and chlorophyll does not establish its cause.

Source roles: NOAA VIIRS supplies the main chlorophyll record. NASA JPL MUR, distributed as a NOAA monthly composite, supplies temperature. A separate NASA MODIS Aqua month checks an aggregation example; it does not validate the long-term trends or guarantee challenge eligibility.

How the trends are estimated

Each estimate fits a linear time trend with a separate effect for each calendar month, so the recurring seasonal cycle is accounted for. Chlorophyll is modelled on a natural-log scale; its rate is expressed as percent change per decade. Temperature is modelled in degrees Celsius and is never a percentage.

The chart's trend averages the fitted calendar-month effects equally, retaining the fitted slope. Chlorophyll's absolute change is anchored to the geometric mean of valid observations in the first 12 calendar months of the selected window. This is concentration context, not an independent absolute-slope model.

The direct regional test fits the difference of EEP and NWP log chlorophyll on months valid in both regions. It tests a relative growth-rate difference. Different individual p-values do not establish a difference between regions.

Intervals and p-values use approximate Student t inference with calendar-spaced Bartlett heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent (HAC) uncertainty. They are sensitive to modelling assumptions. A 95% interval spanning zero leaves the direction inconclusive under this model; it does not prove an absence of change.

A conservative project policy requires at least 120 calendar months and 120 valid observations, at least 85% completeness, five observations for each calendar month, and no gap longer than six months. A month needs at least 10% valid regional coverage. These thresholds are screening rules, not universal guarantees of reliable inference.

Changing dates creates an exploratory investigation. Repeatedly searching windows for small p-values increases the risk of misleading findings. Treat custom-window inference as exploratory.
